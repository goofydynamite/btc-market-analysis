Bitcoin Market Intelligence

I took 14 years of one-minute Bitcoin prices and answered five decisions a retail crypto-investing app has to make: how to talk about cycles, how much risk customers carry, when to run operations, whether to default to recurring buys, and whether to slow down panic selling.

Q1 · Strategy

How do Bitcoin's cycles behave, and where are we now?

Each halving cycle, indexed to 1× on halving day

    Decision

    Stop using "the next cycle will repeat" in marketing. Gains shrink every cycle, so plan growth campaigns around the 12–18-month post-halving window but don't promise its size.

    Q2 · Risk & compliance

    How much risk do customers carry, and is it changing?

    Annualised volatility by year

      Decision

      Recalibrate risk disclosures, collateral haircuts and "typical daily move" copy on a rolling 2-year window. Full-history numbers overstate today's risk by about 2×.

            Decision

            Deprioritise the cool-down screen. It would almost never fire in today's market, and a lower trigger has no evidence behind it. Keep it on the backlog and revisit if 90-day volatility returns above 80%.

            Part 2 · Advanced analysis

            Causal inference, regime models, forecasting and statistical rigor

            Part 1 described what happened. Part 2 tests whether it holds up and what drove it, and that changed two of the recommendations.

            A1 · Causal inference

            Did the January 2024 spot-ETF launch change how Bitcoin trades?

            Weekly metrics two years either side of the launch, with autocorrelation-robust (HAC) tests, a structural-break scan, and 40 placebo event dates.

            Share of weekday volume traded in US hours (14:00–21:00 UTC)

            Before ETFAfter ETFPeriod mean
              Decision

              Don't justify lower risk limits with "ETFs made Bitcoin safer": volatility fell, but the break came 17 months earlier. Do shift ops coverage and campaign timing toward US hours, where activity is growing.

              A2 · Risk modelling

              Which volatility regime are we in, and can we forecast risk?

              3-state Markov-switching model on daily returns, plus a GARCH(1,1) with fat-tailed errors, backtested out of sample from 2020 against realized volatility from minute data.

              Share of days in each regime, by year

              CalmNormalStress

              99% one-day VaR backtest, 2020–2026

                Decision

                Compute margin and collateral VaR with fat-tailed GARCH instead of normal-distribution EWMA. Alert risk and support when the model's stress probability goes above 50%.

                A3 · Forecasting

                Can we forecast next week's trading volume for staffing?

                Every Monday, forecast all 168 hours of the coming week using only data at least a week old. Rolling-origin backtest over 2025–2026.

                One example week: hourly USD volume

                Actual4-week profile, bias-correctedGradient boosting
                  Decision

                  Ship the bias-corrected 4-week seasonal profile as the staffing forecast. It's transparent and as accurate as ML. Hold an on-call buffer for the roughly ±40% daily error instead of chasing model accuracy.

                  A4 · Statistical rigor

                  Which findings are real, and what should a customer expect?

                  Each claim is re-tested with permutation or bootstrap tests that respect weekly and autocorrelated structure, with the false-discovery rate controlled across all tests. A block-bootstrap Monte Carlo then simulates 10,000 four-year recurring-buy plans.

                    Decision · revises part 1

                    Don't show historical "99.7% of 3-year holds were profitable" odds in the app. Show the simulated range with a plain-language chance of loss. It's more honest, and it still makes the case for recurring buys through the smaller downside.
